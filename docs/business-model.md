# Business model — KIR

Independent public-sector market-entry compliance for the Republic of
Kiribati.

- Central Procurement Unit (CPU, a division of the Ministry of Finance
  and Economic Development) public procurement -- Public Procurement
  Act 2019 (s.11 Minister empowered to make Regulations; s.14 CPU
  established; s.17 Central Contract Award Board for High-Value
  Procurement (HVP); s.18 Contract Award Committee, per Procuring
  Entity, for Medium-Value Procurement (MVP); s.39A binds Government-
  owned companies and statutory corporations, inserted by the 2021
  Amendment) plus the Public Procurement Manual 2020's own §7.1.6
  four-tier AUD threshold ladder (VLVP<1,000 / LVP 1,000-9,999 / MVP
  10,000-49,999 / HVP>=50,000) with its own explicit accumulation rule
  for recurring engagements (see `src/marketentry/facts.cljk`);
  e-tendering runs on both the CPU's own portal (www.procurement.gov.ki)
  and a third-party platform, illion TenderLink (New Zealand)
- Ministry of Tourism, Commerce, Industry and Cooperatives (MTCIC),
  Business and Companies Regulatory Division: business/company
  registration under the Companies Ordinance (Cap10A); Foreign
  Investment Act 2018 and its own Foreign Investment Guideline
  (Reserved/Restricted/Prohibited activities)
- Kiribati Tax Division (Ministry of Finance and Economic Development)
  TIN (Tax Identification Number) registration -- requires a Business
  Certificate from the Ministry of Commerce and a Business Operating
  Certificate from the Island Council
- Public Procurement Manual 2020 §7.1.6 "procurement-tier" value-
  threshold gate (FLAGSHIP) -- bars a filing that declares a lower-
  oversight tier (e.g. Procuring-Entity-self-managed VLVP/LVP) than its
  own accumulated declared value actually requires (Contract Award
  Committee for MVP, Central Contract Award Board for HVP)
- Foreign Investment Guideline Schedule 1 "reserved-activity" gate --
  bars a foreign-investor engagement that declares itself active in a
  Kiribati-citizens-only Reserved activity (e.g. taxi transport
  services, traditional Tibuta garment production)
